# A tour of Softwarden

A walk through the product with the synthetic demo data (the Contoso organization). Start a development stack and
load the demo as [DEVELOPMENT.md](DEVELOPMENT.md#run-it-locally) describes (`seed_demo`), or seed a Compose stack
with `SOFTWARDEN_ALLOW_SIMULATED=1` ([DEVELOPMENT.md](DEVELOPMENT.md#run-the-full-stack-in-docker)). Every demo
account's password is `softwarden-demo-2026`: `editor@example.test` (Editor), `admin@example.test` (Admin, also the
IT coordinator) and `viewer@example.test` (Viewer).

The walkthroughs follow the order the features were built in; each stands on its own.

## Demo walkthrough: dependencies and change assessments

Sign in as `editor@example.test`. The scenario is a small payroll chain: Workday HR is depended on by the script Nightly Payroll Export, which Finance Ledger depends on, which Executive Dashboard depends on; Finance Ledger and Budget Planner depend on each other (a cycle); Legacy Timesheets is a retired dependent that Employee Timesheets replaces; "Workday to Finance Ledger payroll feed" is an integration implemented by the export script, and "Workday to Zoom user provisioning" is vendor-native.

1. **Needs attention** (choose All): the ownership gaps and reviews, plus **Links to verify** (the export job's link to Workday HR changed owner since it was confirmed; the ledger's link to the export is past the 90-day interval) and **Stale assessments** (Workday 2027R1 upgrade).
2. **Dependencies:** choose Workday HR, set Depth to All and turn on Include retired. The Table shows each path in dependency order, with the cycle row marked and not followed, the retired dependent tagged, and the two integrations under their own "data flow, not operational" heading. Show path on a row fills the side panel; Graph draws the same result.
3. **Relationships tab** on Finance Ledger: depends on, used by, and the data flows. Add, edit or remove a link, or Confirm link (it needs an Editor or Admin; a Viewer can read and see the history).
4. **Change assessments:** open Workday 2027R1 upgrade. Its banner says why the snapshot is stale; Refresh snapshot makes a new one and keeps the old one in Snapshot history. "Retire Budget Planner" is current; its affected set shows the cycle. New assessment (or Start change assessment on Dependencies) saves another.
5. **Retire with acknowledgment:** edit Workday HR, set Lifecycle to Retired and Save. The dialog lists the active dependents and asks for a reason; the links are kept, and the reason shows on the record and in its Audit activity.
6. **Audit:** a record's Audit activity tab shows its links' events in words, and Show changes on a row opens its before and after values (any role). Administration › Audit log (Admin) names every link and assessment, filters by actor, event, dates, target type, target, parent record and request (all kept in the URL), and each event's page shows its changes side by side with a link to every event from the same request.

## Demo walkthrough: renewals and the digest

Sign in as `editor@example.test` (or `admin@example.test`, the IT coordinator, for the unassigned items).

1. **Renewals:** the list is in action-deadline order. Each deadline says how it was worked out: Finance Ledger support and Zoom Pro have Entered deadlines and are overdue, Workday subscription is Calculated (March 31 minus a two-month notice is January 31), ServiceDesk Plus is Assumed (no notice period, so the 90-day default lead time), Budget Planner license is a Renewal action (it doesn't auto-renew), and Adobe Sign has no deadline at all. Try the quick filters; they live in the URL.
2. **Workday subscription:** the deadline card explains the date and lists the reminders. Cycle history has two completed cycles with their decisions and evidence. Record a decision, then a completion, then Establish next cycle: the new renewal date starts from the one-year term, and the completed cycle moves to history unchanged.
3. **Gaps:** ServiceDesk Plus (unknown auto-renewal, assumed deadline, nobody responsible or following up), Adobe Sign (no deadline, no applications), Legacy Timeclock maintenance (every covered application is retired), Payroll connector license (an entered deadline but no renewal date), and Finance Ledger support (decided, not completed, past the deadline).
4. **Needs attention:** Renewal actions and Renewal gaps sit beside the catalog queues. A row's Notifications menu snoozes it until a date or (for a renewal action) acknowledges it until the next reminder; the row stays, marked. As the Admin, choose Unassigned to see what the coordinator catches.
5. **Digest:** to see one, start Mailpit and the worker with email pointed at it (below), or set the organization's digest time a minute ahead in Organization settings. Administration › Organization settings shows the last run, and View delivery log lists each digest with Retry now. Your own digest can be turned off from your name in the header.

## Demo walkthrough: discovery

Sign in as `editor@example.test` (or `admin@example.test` for connector settings). `seed_demo` adds an Entra connector on the simulated tenant (through its own Microsoft connection, tested), runs it once, and makes a few decisions: Workday and Zoom are linked to their records and a developer's test registration is dismissed. Nothing here contacts Microsoft. The simulated tenant works with `SOFTWARDEN_DEBUG` on (local development), in the Playwright server, or with `SOFTWARDEN_ALLOW_SIMULATED=1` (the Docker demo, and the dev stack sets it); elsewhere `seed_demo` refuses and says so.

1. **Discovery** (in the navigation for every role): the status line shows the last run and its counts. The default filter is New and changed; the other candidates wait for a decision. Link opens a dialog with name-similar records, Create record fills a new application from the candidate, and Dismiss asks for a reason. Every decision has an Undo. Suggested owners are shown matched to People by email, and are never applied: owners change only through an ordinary owner edit.
2. **Skipped and counted:** Microsoft first-party apps and disabled apps are left out by default. The connector's page (Settings on Discovery; Details for other roles) shows its connection and whether it is tested, its scope, its permissions and last access check, and its recent runs with seen, kept and skipped per filter; **Run history** lists every connector's runs with filters in the URL.

   **Guided setup:** as the Admin, choose **Add a source** on Discovery (or open `/discovery/setup`), pick a source and follow the five steps: **Prepare** (what to do in the admin center, with the permissions this source uses and a link to the in-app permissions reference, which is docs/PERMISSIONS.md), **Connect** (choose or add a connection, then test it: a failure says what went wrong and what to do next; the client secret is write-only, "Client secret set on DATE" and a field to replace it), **Scope**, **Check access** (one read per permission; a missing one is named with the fix, optional ones never block) and **Finish** (Run now with live progress, then the counts and what the scope skipped). The step is in the URL, so a reload resumes. **Connections** (Admin) lists the Microsoft and Halo connections (Okta and Google Workspace connections have an API; their screens are an SPA follow-up); one changed since its last test reads "Changed since last test" and no connector runs on it until it is tested again. Azure, Power Platform and Halo show as unavailable in the community edition.
3. **Needs attention:** Discovery candidates is one row per connector ("N new and M changed candidates"), routed to the connector's reviewer, else the coordinator. It appears in the digest too.
4. **Discovery sources** on a record's detail page (Workday HR): the linked observation with its source values, a "Source differs" marker where the curated value differs, and Unlink.

   **Shadow IT (API only so far):** a connector of the source `entra_shadow_it` (`POST /api/v1/connectors` with `"source": "entra_shadow_it"` and the simulated tenant's connection) reads the sign-in logs and OAuth consent grants and makes each third-party app nobody registered a discovery candidate, with its users and sign-ins over 30 days, consent type and high-risk scopes. `GET /api/v1/discovery/shadow-it` is the overview (and `export.csv`). It needs `AuditLog.Read.All` (with Entra ID P1 or P2) and `Directory.Read.All`; without P1, turn off `sign_ins` in its scope to use consent grants only. Only counts are stored, never who signed in ([docs/PERMISSIONS.md](PERMISSIONS.md), "Discovery source: `entra_shadow_it`").

   **SSO and MFA coverage (API only so far):** a connector of the source `entra_coverage` (`"source": "entra_coverage"`, same simulated connection) reads, for every application linked to an Entra app (its Entra application ID, or a linked Entra candidate), whether it is behind single sign-on, whether SCIM provisioning is on, and whether conditional access requires MFA for all users or only some. `GET /api/v1/reports/sso-coverage` is the report (and `export.csv`), the application detail shows it as `sso_coverage` with gap chips, and the data-quality rules No SSO, No MFA policy and No SCIM pick up the gaps. It needs `Application.Read.All`, `Synchronization.Read.All` and `Policy.Read.All` (conditional access needs Entra ID P1; without it MFA shows as unknown and the rest still works). Nothing is written to Entra or to your records ([docs/PERMISSIONS.md](PERMISSIONS.md), "Discovery source: `entra_coverage`").

   **License waste (API only so far):** a connector of the source `entra_licence_usage` (`"source": "entra_licence_usage"`, same simulated connection) reads each licensed user's licenses and last successful sign-in.
   - `GET /api/v1/reports/licence-waste` (and `export.csv`) shows, per SKU, the licenses that are active, inactive (no sign-in for 90 days by default, `inactive_seat_days` in Organization settings), never signed in, held by disabled accounts, or unknown.
   - It also shows the yearly cost tied up in them. The cost comes from the contract of the license pool bound to the SKU, in the contract's currency. A missing price is a named gap, never 0.
   - Admins can drill into one SKU (`GET /api/v1/reports/licence-waste/{sku_id}`), which lists linked people by name and everyone else as "Unlinked user" with a short hash.
   - License pools and contracts have their own block (`.../licence-waste`), and the offboarding footprint lists a person's licenses.

   Only a keyed hash of each user's object ID, their SKU IDs and dates are stored: no names or emails. It needs `User.Read.All`, plus `AuditLog.Read.All` with Entra ID P1 for the last sign-in. Both are tenant-wide admin consents. Without P1 the licenses are still counted and the last sign-in shows as unknown ([docs/PERMISSIONS.md](PERMISSIONS.md), "Discovery source: `entra_licence_usage`").
5. **Change the tenant and see the queue react:**

```bash
cd apps/api
export SOFTWARDEN_DEBUG=1
uv run python manage.py simulate_entra_change rename "Graph Explorer Test" "Graph Explorer Test 2"
uv run python manage.py simulate_entra_change disable Zoom
uv run python manage.py simulate_entra_change add "New Vendor Portal"
uv run python manage.py simulate_entra_change add_registration "Build Pipeline"     # a registration with no enterprise app
uv run python manage.py simulate_entra_change secret "Zoom" "Zoom API" 14           # a client secret ending in 14 days
uv run python manage.py simulate_entra_change remove_credentials "Payroll Sync Daemon"
uv run python manage.py simulate_entra_change tag "Expense Hub" AgentCreatedBy:CopilotStudio   # becomes an AI agent candidate
uv run python manage.py simulate_entra_change reset
```

   Then choose Run now on Discovery (the worker picks the run up within a minute; start `run_worker` if it isn't running). The dismissed registration comes back as changed because its name changed; an unchanged dismissed candidate stays dismissed, and a linked one never re-enters the queue.
6. **Sign in with Microsoft Entra ID**, locally, through the simulated provider (it works only while `SOFTWARDEN_DEBUG` is on, and in the Playwright server). Start the API with these settings, and browse to the same address as `SOFTWARDEN_BASE_URL`:

```bash
cd apps/api
SOFTWARDEN_DEBUG=1 SOFTWARDEN_OIDC_TENANT=simulated SOFTWARDEN_OIDC_CLIENT_ID=demo-client SOFTWARDEN_OIDC_CLIENT_SECRET=demo-secret SOFTWARDEN_BASE_URL=http://localhost:5173 uv run python manage.py runserver 127.0.0.1:8010
```

   The sign-in page then offers "Sign in with Microsoft Entra ID". The first sign-in matches one active user by email and pins the Entra identity to it; no user is ever created, and an unknown identity is refused with a message. Users & roles (Admin) shows "Signs in with Entra" with the issuer and last sign-in, and Unlink (behind a confirmation). Local sign-in keeps working. These `SOFTWARDEN_OIDC_*` settings are the legacy single-organization path; an organization's own settings (`/api/v1/sso/settings`) can use the same simulator with the Entra tenant `simulated`, the Okta domain `okta.simulated.invalid` (authorization server `default`) or the Google preset (all only while `SOFTWARDEN_DEBUG` is on or in the Playwright server; there Google's discovery address is answered by the simulator, never by Google).
7. **Upgrade check:** `uv run python manage.py upgrade_check --write-manifest before.json` records the counts and checksums of reviews, renewal completions and audit events, and every record, contract and membership by id; after an upgrade or restore, `upgrade_check --manifest before.json` runs the post-upgrade checks and compares them, and exits non-zero on any failure. The steps are in [the upgrade runbook](04-development/upgrade-runbook.md).

## Demo walkthrough

Sign in as `editor@example.test` (or `admin@example.test` for import sources and API tokens). `seed_demo` adds an import source named "IT spreadsheet", a few demo tags, a shared saved filter named "Finance apps", and a sample file at `apps/api/catalog/management/commands/sample_csv/applications.csv`. Everything is synthetic; do not import real data.

1. **Imports** (Editors and Admins): New import, kind Applications, source "IT spreadsheet", upload the sample file. Download template shows the fixed columns. A file with the template's headers goes straight to the preview.
2. **Preview:** tiles count Create, Update, Unchanged, Error and Excluded. Updates show before and after per field, and a change to a confirmed field is marked "will need confirmation". Owners are matched by email only, teams and vendors by name; each value that matches nothing offers Create or Leave gap. Departments and software categories are matched by name too but are never created by an import: an unknown one is a row error. Yes/no columns (`sso_enabled`, `scim_provisioning`) take true, false, yes or no; blank leaves the value. A row that looks like an existing record by name (but has no Softwarden ID or external key) is a Possible match and needs a decision: Create new or Match. A new record with a blank lifecycle is a row error; Exclude it, or fix the file.
3. **Commit:** it runs in one transaction. It is refused, with nothing saved and the run marked Refused, if an error row isn't excluded, a choice is missing, or a record changed after the preview; Rerun preview makes a new run. Results link to Needs attention and offer the error report as CSV. Run the same file again: the rows are Unchanged, because the source's external keys now match.
4. **A file that isn't the template** stops at Map columns: each header gets a suggestion, and you map it to a field, the external key, or Ignore (`name` must be mapped). "Save this mapping for" the source stores it for the next run. Import sources (Admin) lists the sources and refuses to delete one that has external keys.
5. **Export:** on Applications or Components choose Export CSV. It uses the list's current filters (and optionally archived records), writes the template columns plus Softwarden ID, and is audited. Cells that start with `=`, `+`, `-` or `@` are escaped, so a name such as `-Internal` is written `'-Internal` and read back unchanged. Import the exported file: everything is Unchanged.
6. **Saved views:** the Saved views menu on the Applications, Components and AI agents lists has four built-ins (Missing owners, Overdue reviews, Unreviewed imports, Active) and the shared "Finance apps". Missing owners is the gap filter, so it also catches a missing support team or an inactive owner. **Save as view…** keeps the current filters and columns, for yourself or shared (Editors and Admins). The open view is in the URL (`?view=<id>`, which alone also opens it) and its columns apply; a changed filter or column is marked "Changed since saved", and the view's owner (or an Admin, for a shared view) can **Save changes**, **Rename or share** and **Delete** it (If-Match: a change someone saved meanwhile asks you to load the latest version). **Email me weekly…** picks the day, says when it is sent ("Sent Wednesdays at 08:00 Pacific/Auckland"), shows the last send and sends a test now; Admins see a shared view's **Subscribers…** and can subscribe a member. Your profile has a **Saved view emails** switch and lists your weekly emails, including those of views since deleted. A weekly email's link (`/applications?<filters>&preset=all&view=<id>`) opens the list with exactly the email's filter (see "Saved views and subscriptions").
7. **Tags and bulk edit:** tags are set on the record form and appear on the detail page and lists; a new name is created on first use. Select up to 200 rows and choose Bulk edit: business owner, technical owner, support team, add or remove tags, and next review date. The dialog previews every change before and after, then applies them in one transaction with one audit event per record. A record with no review can't be bulk-dated, because the date edits the latest review. Bulk edit also sets the IT steward, department, lifecycle, criticality and custom field values (validated as on the record form; retiring a record that active records outside the selection depend on is refused for that record, and dependents selected with it are retired first). Admins can bulk delete up to 200 records of one kind (`POST /api/v1/bulk-delete/preview` and `/apply`): the preview lists what blocks each record (reviews, instances, relationships, integrations, assessments, discovery links), and apply deletes all of them, each with its own audit event and its attachments, or none (409 listing the blocked records).
8. **API tokens** (Administration › API tokens, Admin): create a token and copy it now; it is shown once, begins `sw_`, and only its hash is stored. It needs a name, a read-only choice and an expiry within 365 days. It acts with its owner's role, and every audit event it writes names the token. A token you create for someone else stops (it is revoked) when you are demoted below Admin, deactivated or removed. After you close it, the list shows only its prefix (`sw_` and 8 characters). **API reference** (`/admin/api`, from your name in the sidebar; any role) shows every route from this installation's OpenAPI schema, and **API guide** (`/admin/api/guide`) is docs/API.md.

```bash
curl -H "Authorization: Bearer sw_YOUR_TOKEN" "http://127.0.0.1:8010/api/v1/applications"
```

   A token in a query string or cookie is ignored. An unknown, revoked or expired token, or one whose owner was deactivated, gets 401; a read-only token gets 403 on a write; a token cannot manage tokens. Revoke it on the same page.
9. **Backups:** the worker takes an encrypted set every night at 02:30 UTC (`SOFTWARDEN_BACKUP_CRON`): `pg_dump`, the attachment files and the `upgrade_check` manifest, each encrypted with `openssl` under the `backup_key` the init service generates in the secrets volume, with SHA-256 sums, into the `softwarden-backups` volume, keeping 14 daily and 8 weekly. The System page shows the last good backup's age (a warning after 26 hours) and warns until an Admin confirms the key is copied off the host. `docker compose exec worker python manage.py backup_now --inline` takes one now. `ops/restore.sh` restores a set from the worker or from the host script `ops/backup.sh` (still an alternative), and `ops/restore.sh --rehearse` proves one restores into a scratch database without touching the live stack. The steps, the key and the drill are in [the backup runbook](04-development/backup-runbook.md).

## Getting around

- **Organization switcher:** an account in more than one organization switches at the top of the sidebar (or from the command palette); every page then shows only that organization, other open tabs follow, and each switch is audited in both organizations. With one organization the sidebar just shows its name.
- **Command palette and shortcuts:** Ctrl+K (Cmd+K on a Mac) searches applications, components, AI agents, integrations, people and renewals, jumps to any page and runs actions such as Create application. `?` lists the single-key shortcuts (`g a` Applications, `g n` Needs attention, `c` create, `/` search, `[` collapse the sidebar); turn them off with "Single-key shortcuts" in that dialog or on your Profile.
- **Display preferences per browser:** column choice and row density on the Applications and Components lists, the collapsed sidebar, the single-key setting and the palette's recent records (kind and ID only) are kept in this browser for your account, not on the server.

## Getting around

- **Custom fields** (Admin › Custom fields, `/custom-fields`; every role can open it read-only, only Admins change it): add a field of any type for applications, components, integrations, AI agents and vendors, filter the list by kind and type, edit its label, choices, default, required, weight and reminder flag (the name never changes; the type is locked while records hold values, and removing a choice or kind in use is refused with a count on that field), and delete it (the dialog says how many records lose a value and asks you to confirm). Each field then shows as a typed input in the record, integration and vendor forms (defaults filled in on new records, its description as help, errors from the server on the field itself, focus on the first one), in a **Custom fields** panel on the record and integration pages and a column on the Vendors page, as optional table columns (off until chosen under Columns) and as filters on the Applications, Components and AI agents lists: text contains, choices as chips (any of them), yes / no, numbers and dates exact, from and to, and "is set" for every field, all in the URL. A link with a filter the list no longer takes shows the server's reason and Clear filters; a saved filter that refers to a deleted field says so and opens without it.
- **AI agents** (Catalog › AI agents, `g e`) list, filter (platform, channel, risk, human in the loop, actions can write, model, provider, auth mode and environment, plus the shared filters, all in the URL), export to CSV or Excel, and open each agent's page: its models, channels, governance (data access scope, human-in-loop decision, whether its actions can write), ownership, reviews, relationships and history with diffs. The form edits all of it; an edit sends the record's ETag back as `If-Match`, so a change someone else saved first is refused with a "Load the latest version" choice instead of being overwritten (applications and components work the same way now). Needs attention has an **AI agent gaps** queue (no owner, no data access scope, no human-in-loop decision) next to Ownership gaps. Every link to a record goes through one helper (`apps/web/src/lib/kinds.ts`), so an agent always opens `/ai-agents/<id>`.
- **Reference data under Catalog** (everyone reads; Editors and Admins change): **AI models** with its **Providers** and **Models in use** report, **Software categories** and **Tags** (a color from the palette and a description; tag chips show the color and always the name). A model, provider or category that something uses can't be deleted: the dialog says what uses it and links there.
- **Catalog fields:** applications show and edit category, deployment model, version, primary and admin URLs, authentication method, SSO and SCIM (yes, no or not recorded), and the Entra IDs; components the new types, hosting, environment, schedule, runbook and Azure / Power Platform / external IDs; all three kinds department, cost center and data classification. The useful ones are optional list columns and filter chips. Vendors have support contacts, a portal, an account manager and notes; teams have optional members.
- **Compliance profile:** in an edition that includes it, Organization settings has a "Use the compliance profile" switch (Admins). While it is on, record forms and pages show GxP relevance, validation status and document, periodic review dates and risk tier; while off they are hidden and their values kept. In community the switch isn't shown and never sent.

## Getting around

- **Relationship types** (Admin › Relationship types, `/relationship-types`; every role can open it read-only, only Admins change it): each type's name (how a link reads from its source, "Depends on"), inverse name (from its target, "Is needed by"), slug, whether it counts as a dependency, Core or Added, and how many relationships use it; search and the Dependency and Origin filters are in the URL. Admins add a type (the slug is made from the name unless typed, and never changes), rename and describe any type, switch "Counts as a dependency" on an added one, and delete an added type nobody uses; a type in use explains how many links use it and lists them (each opens its source record's Relationships tab) instead of offering a delete. Core types can't be deleted and their slug and dependency setting are fixed.
- **Links read both ways:** every relationship is labeled from the API's type names: a record's Relationships tab shows it under the type's name from the source and under the inverse name from the target (Depends on, Is needed by, Replaces / is replaced by, Implements, and a section for each added type once a link uses it), and the confirmation and remove dialogs, Needs attention, the dependency explorer's paths ("Portal → depends on → Sync") and change assessments' affected-set paths use the same words. The Add relationship picker lists the organization's types (dependency types first, each both ways, with the type's description as help), and editing a link keeps its type and direction.
- **Integration fields:** the integration form has Direction, Authentication and Data classification (each with "Not recorded") and a Monitoring URL; the integration page shows them by label (the monitoring URL as a link that opens in a new tab, a dash when not recorded); the Integrations list has an Authentication column, a ⇄ arrow for two-way flows, and Direction, Authentication and Data classification filters (any of the chosen values, comma-separated in the URL).
- **Map** (a tab on every application, component and AI agent): everything the record is connected to (its relationships both ways, integrations, instances and the discovery sources it was linked to or created from) as a picture with a table beside it (below it on narrower screens). The table is grouped as the API groups connections (Dependencies, Other relationships, Integrations, Instances, Discovery sources), each heading with the record's own count; each row reads from the record ("Depends on", "Is needed by", "Exchanges data with") and links to the other end (records and integrations to their pages, an instance to its application's Instances tab, a discovery source to Discovery filtered to it). **1 step / 2 steps** (`depth=2`), the selected item (`node`) and an open answer (`dependents`) are in the URL. Click an item on the picture, or focus the picture and use the arrow keys and Enter or Space (Escape clears the focus ring, Tab leaves the picture), or choose **Show on map** in the table, to select it: it is highlighted in both, with **Open**, **Center map here** (that record's own map) and **What depends on this**. **What depends on this** (also one click from each record's row, and from **What depends on <record>** beside 1 step / 2 steps for the record itself, for example when its app registration is recorded on the application) lists every record that depends on it at any depth, with the path, from the record's own `/dependencies?depth=all`, and links to the explorer for more; closing a panel puts focus back on the button that opened it. Archived and retired items have a dashed outline and the word; a map that stops at 150 items says so. Viewers can use all of it.
- **Dependencies on Cytoscape:** the explorer's Graph view is now an interactive picture (zoom, fit, click or arrow keys and Enter to select a record for the path panel), drawn in columns by distance as before; depth is a list from 1 to 10 levels or All levels; a link that leads back to a record already on the path is marked "Closes a loop" (↻ and the words in the table and path panel, a dash-dot line on the picture); data flows show their direction (⇄ Both ways) and authentication. Every filter and the target are in the URL, so the Map tab can link straight to `/dependencies?target=<id>&depth=all`. Printing the Graph view prints the full layered picture, which also stands in if the interactive one can't load.
- **Bulk edit, more fields** (select rows on Applications, Components or AI agents, then **Bulk edit…**): besides owners, support team, tags and next review date, the dialog sets the IT steward (Admins and Editors), department, lifecycle and criticality (each with Leave unchanged, and Clear where a value can be blank) and custom field values: choose a field from **Change a custom field**, then its value in the same input as the record form (blank or Not set clears it on every selected record; **Don't change** takes it back out). The preview lists each record's fields before and after (custom fields by their label) and how many will need confirmation (an owner, the support team, lifecycle or criticality moving away from what the last review confirmed). A bad custom field value is shown on its input and in the summary, as on the record form; a record that can't take the change says why on its row, with the field it refused, and nothing is saved. Retiring a record that active records outside the selection depend on is refused on its row, with links to retire it on its own page (where the dependents can be acknowledged) and to what depends on it; selecting the dependents too retires them all, dependents first.
- **Bulk delete** (Admins; **Bulk delete…** beside Bulk edit): the dialog checks every selected record first and lists which can be deleted (and the attachments and data-quality exceptions that go with each) and which are blocked, by what ("Referenced by 1 instance, 1 relationship. Archive it instead."). Nothing is offered while any is blocked: **Deselect blocked** takes those out of the selection and checks the rest again. Deleting needs a ticked confirmation naming the count; each record gets its own audit event. If another record started referencing one in the meantime, nothing is deleted and that row turns Blocked. Editors and Viewers don't see the action.
- **History with related items:** a record's **Audit activity** tab (applications, components, AI agents, integrations, renewals) also lists the events on its attachments, instances, reviews, relationships (read from this record's end: "Linked to Sync: depends on" here, "Linked to Portal: is needed by" on Sync), link confirmations, integrations, data-quality exceptions and renewal cycles, each with its kind ("Attachment", "Relationship"; "Related" for anything newer) and a sentence. **Only this record** keeps the record's own events (`children=false` in the URL).
- **Audit log export** (Admin › Audit log): **Export CSV** downloads the log with the filters on screen (newest first, at most 100,000 events per file; with more, the page says how many matched and **Choose dates** takes you to From to narrow the range). With the enterprise `audit.export` capability, **Export for SIEM (JSON lines)** downloads the same events oldest first with their before and after snapshots. Both exports are recorded in the log.

## Getting around

- **Contracts** (Money › Contracts, `g k`; with the `contracts` capability, in every edition): every agreement with its vendor, status (a chip with a glyph and the word: Signed, Active, Pending renewal, Renewed, Canceled, Expired), annualized cost in its own currency, end date, cancellation deadline (with how it was worked out and how far off it is), the renewal it is linked to and its cost center; contract number, lines, license pools, renewal owner and custom fields are optional columns. Search, the quick filters (deadline in the next 90 days, not linked to a renewal, archived) and the filter chips (status, vendor, billing frequency, auto-renewal, renewal, deadline and end date ranges, cost center, currency, archived and every custom field) are in the URL; **Export CSV** and **Export Excel** download what is on screen.
- **Add or edit a contract:** name, vendor (added on the spot if new), renewal owner, contract and PO numbers, the amount (typed as text with up to two decimals, never rounded through a floating-point number) with its currency and billing frequency, cost center, start and end dates, auto-renewal (yes, no or unknown), the notice period and the status. While you type, the form shows the annualized cost and the cancellation deadline the API works out (`GET /contracts/preview`). **Renewal:** leave it unlinked, link an existing renewal (one whose dates already come from another contract says so, and linking it is refused in the API's words) or create one for the contract. An edit sends only what changed, with the contract's ETag: if someone saved first, the form says so and **Load the latest version** reloads it.
- **The contract page:** Overview (the deadline and its explanation; "Dates for renewal X come from this contract" when it drives its renewal, or which contract does), cost and term, notes and custom fields; **Lines** (each record's fixed share or "Shares the rest", its share of the cost and amount a year, the fixed total and what is not allocated; **Add line**, **Change share** and **Remove**, with the total the change would give shown before saving and the API's "over 100%" refusal on the field); **Renewal** (every cycle with its decision, completion and what the renew decision changed); **License pools**; **Attachments**; **History** (its own events and its lines' and attachments', with **Only this record**). "Renews X" and "Renewed by Y" link the terms of one agreement. Archive and restore from More (Editors), delete (Admins; refused while a pool or next term points at it).
- **Renew or cancel…** (`/contracts/<id>/renew`, Editors): starts from what the API proposes (the next term starts the day after this one ends and runs as long, same money and notice, every line and pool). Choose Renew, Renegotiate or Cancel (notes required to cancel), change any next-term field, untick lines or pools, move or copy the pools, and confirm an overlap if the next term starts before this one ends. A refusal (already renewed, canceled, archived, no end date) is shown in words; a field the API refuses, including a custom field made required since, is marked on the form so it can be fixed there. Renewing opens the next term; the renewal's cycle is completed and its next cycle takes the new dates.
- **Renewals driven by a contract:** the renewal page says "Dates come from the contract X" (a link), and its Edit terms dialog explains that the renewal date, notice period and cancellation deadline follow the contract; changing them there is refused with the API's reason on the field. Cycle history shows what each renew decision changed (money and end date before and after).
- **License pools** (Money › License pools): seat totals for the organization (purchased, assigned, utilization, overused application and SKU rows), then each pool's application, contract, utilization (a small bar plus "90 of 100 seats (90%)"; overuse is "! Overused by N seats", never color alone), seat sync and how many proposed counts wait; filters (overuse, seat sync, contract, application, custom fields) and the CSV export follow the URL. The pool page shows the same with its SKU ID and the read-only last sync date; the form refuses seat sync without a SKU ID.
- **Proposed seat counts** (the **Proposed seat counts** tab of the list and of each pool, `?seats=open|dismissed|all`): where a discovery source reports other purchased or assigned seats for a pool's SKU ID, each difference is listed with **Apply…** (choose the counts; an audited pool edit naming the source) and **Dismiss…** (a reason is required; it holds until the source's counts change). Dismissed proposals keep their reason, who and when. SKUs no pool tracks are listed for information with **Add pool**.
- **Needs attention › Seat counts:** one row per pool with open proposals, routed to its application's IT steward, linking to the pool's review; it counts in the badge and goes into the daily digest like the other queues, and can be snoozed.
- **Palette, search and Map:** contracts are a group in the command palette and the search results (and in Recent), "Create contract" and "Create license pool" are palette actions (`c` on their pages), and a record's Map lists its contracts (with the line's share, status and end date) and an application's license pools (seats in words), each linking to its page.
- **Dashboard** (Work › Dashboard, `g b`; every role, read-only): one page from `GET /dashboard`. **Needs attention**: the counts assigned to you, in all and unassigned, and each queue's size; every number opens that queue on Needs attention in that scope (`/?scope=all#h-reviews`, the heading focused); a queue not yet in the badge says so. **Deadlines**: overdue and due within 30, 60 and 90 days as toggles (`due` in the URL) over the 10 soonest, each linking to its renewal or contract, with how the date was worked out (a stored date its computation doesn't explain shows its words alone) and the **Renewals calendar** for the rest. **Spend**: per currency (a currency switch with more than one; amounts are never converted or added across currencies), the annualized total, run rate, one-time charges and Signed terms not started yet, then by vendor, department or category (tabs, `spend` and `currency` in the URL) as a bar chart with the same rows in a table: the largest 8 groups, every named gap (⚠ No vendor, Not on a record…) and "… Everything else". **License utilization** (overuse in words), **Expiring credentials** (API tokens expiring within 30 days or expired in the last 30: an Admin sees the organization's, others their own) and **Data quality** (the panel). A block the edition lacks says "isn't available", never zero. **Everyone's home:** every role lands on the Dashboard after sign-in (password or Entra), first-run setup and an organization switch, and it leads Work in the navigation with Needs attention second; `/` stays Needs attention (a link asked for at sign-in wins; Entra sign-in returns to the requested page, `/dashboard` when none was asked for).
- **Reports** (Money › Reports, `g x`; every role): **Spend** (with `contracts`): per currency, grouped **By vendor**, **By department**, **By category** or **By cost center** (`tab=spend&by=…` in the URL), the groups with named gaps, and the **Allocation detail** (each contract line's share and annual amount, and each contract's Not allocated rest, with the group it counts under). **Renewals calendar**: **Months ahead** (1 to 24, `months` in the URL), the overdue deadlines first, a count per month and every deadline coming up, from the dates reminders use. **License utilization** (with `contracts`): seats per application and SKU and the organization's total. Each has **Export CSV** (recorded in the audit log).
- **Calendar subscription** (Reports › Renewals calendar): **Create calendar feed** makes your own address for these deadlines as a calendar (one all-day event per deadline). The https address is shown **once**, with **Copy address**: paste it into Outlook (**Add calendar**, **Subscribe from web**, paste, **Import**; classic Outlook: **Add Calendar**, **From Internet**), Google Calendar (**Other calendars**, **+**, **From URL**) or Apple Calendar (**File**, **New Calendar Subscription**; on iPhone **Settings**, **Calendar**, **Accounts**, **Add Account**, **Other**, **Add Subscribed Calendar**). Afterwards the panel shows the address's prefix, when it was made and when a calendar last fetched it. **Rotate address…** warns that every calendar subscribed to the current address stops updating, then shows the new address once; **Revoke…** stops it. A lost address can't be shown again: rotate instead. No webcal link is offered (calendar apps fetch those over plain http first).
- **Importing contracts and license pools** (Imports, with `contracts`): the upload step offers **Contracts** and **License pools** with their own template and minimum columns; the preview shows, per contract row, the annualized cost, the cancellation deadline with its label and explanation, and how the cost splits over the lines ("Not allocated" for the rest), and per pool row the utilization ("12 of 10 seats (120%)", "! Overused by 2"); the service refusals (fixed shares over 100 %, a renewal another contract drives) are row errors under their column; a file over 500 rows says its contract rules are checked at commit; the column mapping names the required column (the SKU name for pools); rows and results link to `/contracts/<id>` and `/license-pools/<id>`, and the results offer **Open Contracts** or **Open License pools**. Import sources can be made for contracts and pools too.
- **Smaller changes:** a contract that can't be renewed now turns Renew and Renegotiate off and points to the reason; the license pool form and the list's Contract filter search contracts as you type (no 200-contract cap); a refusal on a contract line that names no field shown is said in the dialog; money reads with thousands separators everywhere ("12,006.00 USD"); the relationship-type in-use dialog lists the first 20 links and says "Showing 20 of N"; the assessment page words its paths from the snapshot's own type names.
